HAZWOPER Basics

HAZWOPER stands for Hazardous Waste Operations and Emergency Response, the OSHA standard at 29 CFR 1910.120. It sets training requirements for three categories of work: hazardous waste site cleanup, operations at treatment, storage, and disposal facilities (TSDFs), and hazardous materials emergency response. See our full guide for the details.

Hazardous waste sites and emergency response situations can involve toxic exposure, fire, explosion, and other serious hazards. Training scoped to your actual role helps you recognize those hazards and respond correctly, and helps your employer meet OSHA's training requirements.

No. HAZWOPER specifically covers the three categories above. Other standards apply to different hazardous materials work, such as HazCom for chemical labeling and safety data sheets, or DOT/RCRA rules for transporting hazardous waste. Confirm which standard(s) apply to your specific work.

Training Tracks

Workers who regularly perform hazardous substance removal, containment, or cleanup at a site covered by 1910.120(e), and who could be exposed above permissible exposure limits, commonly need the full 40-hour course, plus at least three days of supervised field experience.

Two groups commonly qualify: workers on-site occasionally for a specific limited task who are unlikely to exceed exposure limits, and employees at a permitted TSD facility whose regular duties fall under 1910.120(p). See our 24-hour training breakdown.

Both cover similar core hazard-recognition and safety topics, but the 40-hour track is scoped for workers with regular, ongoing exposure potential, while the 24-hour track is scoped for occasional exposure or specific TSDF operations. Field experience requirements also differ (commonly three days vs. one day supervised).

Yes. Site supervisors and managers commonly need an additional 8 hours of supervisor-specific training covering program oversight, on top of (not instead of) their own worker-level 40-hour or 24-hour certification.

Emergency responders are trained by response level under 1910.120(q), from Awareness through Incident Commander. Many teams pair 24-hour HAZWOPER emergency response training with 8-hour Incident Command training for personnel who direct the response.

Refresher & Renewal

Commonly, yes. Anyone already HAZWOPER-certified typically needs 8 hours of refresher training annually to keep their certification current, timed from their initial training or last refresher date.

Depending on your employer's policy and how long the lapse has been, you may be able to take the standard 8-hour refresher, or you may need to retake the full initial 40-hour or 24-hour course. Check with your employer or training provider.

No, they commonly take a refresher matched to their original track: a supervisor refresher for supervisors, and a TSDF-specific refresher for TSDF workers, rather than the general 8-hour refresher.
