The Basics

What Is HAZWOPER?

HAZWOPER stands for Hazardous Waste Operations and Emergency Response. It's the OSHA standard at 29 CFR 1910.120 that sets training requirements for three broad categories of work.

🧪

Hazardous Waste Site Cleanup

Investigation, removal, or remediation of hazardous substances at cleanup sites, covered under 1910.120(e).

🏭

TSD Facility Operations

Routine operations at a permitted hazardous waste Treatment, Storage, or Disposal Facility, covered under 1910.120(p).

🚨

Emergency Response

Responding to releases (or threatened releases) of hazardous substances, covered under 1910.120(q).

Why it matters: The training track that commonly applies to you depends on which of these three categories your work falls under, plus your specific duties and exposure potential — not just your job title. When in doubt, check with your employer's safety program or a qualified safety professional.

Cleanup Site Workers

40-Hour HAZWOPER Training

Most Common Initial Track

Who commonly needs it

Workers who regularly perform hazardous substance removal, containment, or cleanup at a site covered by 1910.120(e) — including general site workers, equipment operators, and technicians who could be exposed to hazardous substances above permissible exposure limits.

  • 40 hours of initial classroom and hands-on instruction
  • Minimum of three days of supervised field experience before working unsupervised
  • Covers hazard recognition, PPE selection and use, site control, decontamination, and monitoring
  • Typically followed by an 8-hour annual refresher

Occasional & TSDF Workers

24-Hour HAZWOPER Training

Reduced-Hour Track

Who commonly needs it

Two groups typically qualify for the 24-hour track instead of the full 40 hours: workers who are on-site occasionally for a specific limited task and are unlikely to exceed exposure limits, and employees at a permitted TSD facility whose regular duties are covered under 1910.120(p).

  • 24 hours of initial classroom and hands-on instruction
  • One day of supervised field experience is commonly expected
  • TSDF-specific 24-hour training is a distinct track from the general occasional-worker track
  • Followed by an 8-hour annual refresher matched to the same track

Annual Renewal

8-Hour Refresher Training

Is an 8-hour refresher required every year? Commonly, yes — here's how it works.

When Refresher Training Is Required

Who commonly needs it

Anyone already HAZWOPER-certified under the 40-hour, 24-hour, or TSDF track commonly needs 8 hours of refresher training annually to keep that certification current. The refresher should match the original track: general, TSDF operations, or supervisor.

  • 8 hours annually, timed from the anniversary of initial or prior refresher training
  • Reviews hazard recognition, PPE, decontamination, and any site or regulatory changes
  • A lapsed refresher can mean re-taking the full initial course, depending on your employer's policy
  • Supervisors and TSDF workers should take the refresher matching their original track

Site Leadership

Supervisor Training

Additional 8 Hours

Who commonly needs it

Anyone who supervises HAZWOPER operations — site supervisors, foremen, and safety managers — commonly needs 8 additional hours of supervisor-specific training covering program oversight, in addition to (not instead of) their own 40-hour or 24-hour worker training.

  • Builds on the underlying worker-level HAZWOPER certification
  • Covers site safety plans, PPE program oversight, and emergency response coordination
  • Renewed with a matching supervisor-track 8-hour annual refresher

Emergency Response

Incident Command Training

Emergency Response (q)

Who commonly needs it

1910.120(q) sets tiered training levels for emergency responders, from First Responder Awareness through Incident Commander. Personnel who direct or control an emergency response commonly need incident command training on top of their response-level HAZWOPER training.

  • Commonly paired with 24-hour HAZWOPER emergency response training
  • Covers command structure, resource management, and multi-agency coordination
  • FEMA-aligned ICS 100 and ICS 200 courses are a common complement for broader incident management
