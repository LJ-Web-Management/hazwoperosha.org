# Hazmat Field Safety Quiz: Drums, Confined Spaces and Spills

Free practice quiz · 8 questions

Answer every question, then press **Check my answers** to see your score and an explanation for each one. Study first with the guide: [Drum and Container Handling at Hazardous Waste Sites](https://www.hazwoperosha.org/guides/drum-and-container-handling/).

**1. How should an unlabeled drum at a cleanup site be treated?**

- As empty
- As non-hazardous
- As hazardous until identified
- As water

**Answer: As hazardous until identified.** HAZWOPER requires unlabeled drums to be treated as hazardous until characterized.

**2. Crystals around a drum bung may indicate:**

- Rust only
- Shock-sensitive or peroxide-forming material
- An empty drum
- Normal aging

**Answer: Shock-sensitive or peroxide-forming material.** Treat the drum as potentially explosive and get specialist help.

**3. What should be on hand before moving drums that may leak?**

- Nothing
- Overpack drums, absorbent and a spill containment plan
- Only a forklift
- A camera

**Answer: Overpack drums, absorbent and a spill containment plan.** 1910.120(j) requires salvage drums, absorbent and spill containment for the volume being moved.

**4. In a confined space, which should be tested first?**

- Toxic gases
- Combustible gases
- Oxygen
- Temperature

**Answer: Oxygen.** Test oxygen first, then combustibles, then toxics.

**5. The confined space attendant should:**

- Enter to help whenever needed
- Stay outside, monitor entrants and summon rescue
- Leave once entrants are inside
- Sign the permit as entry supervisor

**Answer: Stay outside, monitor entrants and summon rescue.** Attendants never enter for rescue unless relieved and trained for it.

**6. A small spill that workers in the area can safely absorb with no significant hazard is:**

- An emergency release
- An incidental release
- A reportable quantity
- A TSDF operation

**Answer: An incidental release.** Incidental releases are not HAZWOPER emergency response.

**7. Where do you report a release at or above a CERCLA reportable quantity?**

- OSHA area office
- National Response Center, 1-800-424-8802
- The SDS manufacturer only
- Local newspaper

**Answer: National Response Center, 1-800-424-8802.** Report immediately to the National Response Center and follow your emergency plan.

**8. A retrieval system with a mechanical device is required for vertical permit spaces deeper than:**

- 2 feet
- 5 feet
- 10 feet
- 20 feet

**Answer: 5 feet.** 1910.146(k)(3)(ii) requires a mechanical retrieval device for vertical spaces more than 5 feet deep.
