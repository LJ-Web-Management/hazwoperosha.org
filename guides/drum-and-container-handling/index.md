# Drum and Container Handling at Hazardous Waste Sites

Free lesson · 2 min read · Updated 2026-10-30

Drums are where many of the worst accidents at cleanup sites start: a corroded drum splits when lifted, an unknown liquid reacts with another, or a bulging drum ruptures when someone loosens the bung. Paragraph (j) of the HAZWOPER standard sets out rules for handling, transporting, labeling and disposing of drums and containers.

## Before moving anything

- **Inspect first.** Drums and containers must be inspected and their integrity confirmed before they are moved. Look for corrosion, bulging, leaks, crystals around bungs, and labels or markings.
- **Treat unlabeled drums as hazardous** until their contents are identified and characterized.
- **Keep handling to a minimum.** Move drums only as much as the work requires.
- **Brief the crew** on the hazards before work starts, and keep the area clear of non-essential people.

## Equipment and spill readiness

- Use drum-handling equipment rated for the job, with operator cabs protected against vapors, splashes and explosions where needed.
- Keep **salvage (overpack) drums**, suitable absorbent and DOT-specification containers ready where spills or leaks may occur.
- Have a **spill containment program** ready to stop and contain the entire volume being moved.
- Use non-sparking tools and grounding where flammable vapors may be present.

## Drums that need special care

| Condition | Why it's dangerous | Typical precautions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bulging or swollen | Pressure buildup from reaction or heat can cause rupture | Don't move unless necessary; vent remotely or with shielding; keep people back |
| Leaking or corroded | Releases contents and may fail when lifted | Overpack or transfer contents; contain spills first |
| Crystals around the bung or lid | May indicate shock-sensitive or peroxide-forming material | Treat as possibly explosive; get specialist help |
| Laboratory packs | Many small containers of mixed chemicals | Only opened by trained, experienced personnel |
| Radioactive markings or readings | Radiation exposure | Stop and get a radiation safety officer |

## Opening and sampling

- Open drums remotely or from behind a barrier where the contents are unknown or pressure is suspected.
- Monitor the air as the drum is opened, and stay upwind.
- Keep the people doing the work to the minimum needed, with backup personnel and emergency equipment nearby.
- Never stand on drums, and don't open more drums than you can sample at one time.

## Staging and bulking

Group drums by compatibility, not convenience. Characterize the contents (often with field hazard categorization tests) before bulking any wastes together, and keep incompatible groups apart: acids from bases, oxidizers from flammables and organics, water-reactives away from water. Label each drum with its staging category.

## Buried drums

Before excavation, locate buried drums with methods such as ground-penetrating radar or magnetometers so equipment doesn't puncture them. Dig carefully once you're close. Check your knowledge with the [field safety quiz](https://www.hazwoperosha.org/quizzes/hazmat-field-safety-quiz/).

## Frequently asked questions

### How should unlabeled drums be treated at a hazardous waste site?

As containing hazardous substances until their contents are identified and characterized.

### What should be ready before moving drums that may leak?

Salvage (overpack) drums, suitable absorbent, appropriate containers and a spill containment plan for the full volume being moved.

### What do crystals around a drum bung indicate?

They may indicate shock-sensitive or peroxide-forming material. Treat the drum as potentially explosive and get specialist help.
