HAZWOPER Jobs and Careers: Who Hires, What You Need and How to Start

On this page
  1. Jobs that commonly require HAZWOPER
  2. What employers usually look for
  3. How to get started
  4. Moving up

A 40-hour HAZWOPER certificate is the entry ticket to a wide range of field jobs in environmental cleanup, emergency response and waste management. Here's where it's used and how people get started.

Jobs that commonly require HAZWOPER

JobWhat the work involvesTypical training
Environmental technicianSampling soil, water and air; running monitoring wells; assisting with site investigations40 Hour; sometimes 24 Hour for limited tasks
Remediation laborer or equipment operatorExcavating contaminated soil, operating treatment systems, demolition on cleanup sites40 Hour + field experience
Emergency spill responderResponding to highway, rail, industrial and marine spills for response contractors40 Hour, plus technician-level emergency response training
TSDF operatorReceiving, treating, storing and shipping hazardous waste at permitted facilities24 Hour TSD + annual refresher; RCRA and DOT training
Industrial cleaning technicianTank cleaning, vacuum truck and hydro-blasting work around hazardous residuesOften 40 Hour, plus confined space training
Geologist, engineer or field scientistOverseeing drilling, investigations and cleanup as a consultant40 Hour; supervisors add 8 Hour Supervisor
Facility emergency response team memberResponding to releases at a plant, refinery or warehouseEmergency response training to the assigned level
Site safety officerRunning the site safety and health plan, monitoring and briefings40 Hour + 8 Hour Supervisor + experience

What employers usually look for

  • A current HAZWOPER certificate at the right level, and a refresher within the last 12 months
  • Willingness to wear respirators and protective clothing, and to pass the related medical evaluation and fit test
  • A valid driver's license; some response jobs want a commercial license with a hazmat endorsement
  • For work at ports and some facilities, a Transportation Worker Identification Credential (TWIC)
  • Comfort with physical work outdoors in all weather
  • Extras that stand out: confined space, first aid and CPR, forklift, and Spanish-language skills

How to get started

  1. Take the 40 hour HAZWOPER course (also available in Spanish). It qualifies you for the widest range of entry-level roles.
  2. Put your certificate and completion date at the top of your resume.
  3. Apply to environmental consulting firms, remediation contractors, spill response companies, waste management companies and industrial cleaning firms.
  4. Your employer arranges your supervised field days, medical evaluation and fit testing.
  5. Keep current with an 8 hour refresher every year.

Moving up

Experienced workers move into crew lead and site supervisor roles (with the 8 hour supervisor course), site safety officer roles, project management, or training others as an in-house instructor with train-the-trainer certification. Pay varies a lot by region, employer, travel and overtime, so compare current job postings in your area.

Frequently asked questions

What jobs require HAZWOPER certification?

Environmental technicians, remediation laborers and equipment operators, emergency spill responders, TSDF operators, industrial cleaning technicians, field scientists on cleanup sites, and facility emergency response team members, among others.

Which HAZWOPER course should I take to get a job?

The 40 hour course qualifies you for the widest range of entry-level field jobs. Employers then arrange supervised field experience.

Do I need a refresher to stay employable?

Yes. Employers expect a refresher within the last 12 months, since OSHA requires 8 hours of refresher training every year.
