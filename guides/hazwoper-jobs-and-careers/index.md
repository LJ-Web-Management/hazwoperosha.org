# HAZWOPER Jobs and Careers: Who Hires, What You Need and How to Start

Free lesson · 2 min read · Updated 2026-10-30

A 40-hour HAZWOPER certificate is the entry ticket to a wide range of field jobs in environmental cleanup, emergency response and waste management. Here's where it's used and how people get started.

## Jobs that commonly require HAZWOPER

| Job | What the work involves | Typical training |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Environmental technician | Sampling soil, water and air; running monitoring wells; assisting with site investigations | 40 Hour; sometimes 24 Hour for limited tasks |
| Remediation laborer or equipment operator | Excavating contaminated soil, operating treatment systems, demolition on cleanup sites | 40 Hour + field experience |
| Emergency spill responder | Responding to highway, rail, industrial and marine spills for response contractors | 40 Hour, plus technician-level emergency response training |
| TSDF operator | Receiving, treating, storing and shipping hazardous waste at permitted facilities | 24 Hour TSD + annual refresher; RCRA and DOT training |
| Industrial cleaning technician | Tank cleaning, vacuum truck and hydro-blasting work around hazardous residues | Often 40 Hour, plus confined space training |
| Geologist, engineer or field scientist | Overseeing drilling, investigations and cleanup as a consultant | 40 Hour; supervisors add 8 Hour Supervisor |
| Facility emergency response team member | Responding to releases at a plant, refinery or warehouse | Emergency response training to the assigned level |
| Site safety officer | Running the site safety and health plan, monitoring and briefings | 40 Hour + 8 Hour Supervisor + experience |

## What employers usually look for

- A current HAZWOPER certificate at the right level, and a refresher within the last 12 months
- Willingness to wear respirators and protective clothing, and to pass the related medical evaluation and fit test
- A valid driver's license; some response jobs want a commercial license with a hazmat endorsement
- For work at ports and some facilities, a Transportation Worker Identification Credential (TWIC)
- Comfort with physical work outdoors in all weather
- Extras that stand out: confined space, first aid and CPR, forklift, and Spanish-language skills

## How to get started

1. Take the [40 hour HAZWOPER course](https://www.hazwoperosha.org/courses/osha-40-hour-hazwoper-training/) (also available [in Spanish](https://www.hazwoperosha.org/courses/osha-40-hour-hazwoper-training-spanish/)). It qualifies you for the widest range of entry-level roles.
2. Put your certificate and completion date at the top of your resume.
3. Apply to environmental consulting firms, remediation contractors, spill response companies, waste management companies and industrial cleaning firms.
4. Your employer arranges your supervised field days, medical evaluation and fit testing.
5. Keep current with an [8 hour refresher](https://www.hazwoperosha.org/courses/osha-8-hour-hazwoper-refresher-training/) every year.

## Moving up

Experienced workers move into crew lead and site supervisor roles (with the [8 hour supervisor course](https://www.hazwoperosha.org/courses/osha-8-hour-hazwoper-supervisor-training/)), site safety officer roles, project management, or training others as an in-house instructor with [train-the-trainer certification](https://www.hazwoperosha.org/courses/hazwoper-train-the-trainer-certification/). Pay varies a lot by region, employer, travel and overtime, so compare current job postings in your area.

## Frequently asked questions

### What jobs require HAZWOPER certification?

Environmental technicians, remediation laborers and equipment operators, emergency spill responders, TSDF operators, industrial cleaning technicians, field scientists on cleanup sites, and facility emergency response team members, among others.

### Which HAZWOPER course should I take to get a job?

The 40 hour course qualifies you for the widest range of entry-level field jobs. Employers then arrange supervised field experience.

### Do I need a refresher to stay employable?

Yes. Employers expect a refresher within the last 12 months, since OSHA requires 8 hours of refresher training every year.

This guide summarizes federal OSHA, Cal/OSHA and EPA rules for training purposes. It is not legal advice. Always check the current regulation text and your site safety and health plan.
