# Respirator Selection and Fit Testing for HAZWOPER Work

Free lesson · 2 min read · Updated 2026-10-30

Respirators are covered by their own OSHA standard, **29 CFR 1910.134**, which applies on top of HAZWOPER. Any employer that requires respirators needs a written respiratory protection program run by a qualified program administrator.

## Before anyone wears a respirator

1. **Medical evaluation** by a physician or other licensed health care professional, using the OSHA questionnaire or an equivalent exam, to confirm the worker can breathe through one.
2. **Fit test** with the exact make, model, style and size of tight-fitting facepiece the worker will use.
3. **Training** on why the respirator is needed, its limits, how to put it on, check it, maintain it and store it.

## Two families of respirators

- **Air-purifying respirators (APRs)** filter or absorb contaminants from the surrounding air: filtering facepieces, half and full-face elastomeric masks with cartridges, and powered air-purifying respirators (PAPRs). They need at least 19.5% oxygen and can't be used in IDLH atmospheres.
- **Atmosphere-supplying respirators** provide clean air from a separate source: self-contained breathing apparatus (SCBA) or supplied-air (airline) respirators. These are used for oxygen-deficient, IDLH or unknown atmospheres.

## Assigned protection factors

An **assigned protection factor (APF)** is the level of protection a properly working, properly fitted respirator is expected to give. OSHA's table in 1910.134(d)(3)(i)(A) includes:

| Respirator | APF |
| --- | --- |
| Filtering facepiece or elastomeric half mask (air-purifying) | 10 |
| Full facepiece air-purifying | 50 |
| PAPR with loose-fitting facepiece | 25 |
| PAPR with full facepiece | 1,000 |
| SCBA, pressure-demand, full facepiece | 10,000 |

## Maximum use concentration

The **maximum use concentration (MUC)** is the highest airborne concentration a respirator can be used in. Multiply the APF by the exposure limit. A half mask (APF 10) against a vapor with an exposure limit of 50 ppm has an MUC of 500 ppm. If air monitoring shows higher readings, step up to a respirator with a higher APF. Never use an APR above the IDLH concentration, whatever the math says.

## Filters and cartridges

- **Particulate filters** are rated N (not oil resistant), R (somewhat oil resistant) or P (strongly oil resistant), with efficiency 95, 99 or 100 (99.97%). P100 filters are magenta.
- **Chemical cartridges** are matched to the contaminant: organic vapor cartridges are black, acid gas white, ammonia/methylamine green, and combination cartridges carry more than one color.
- Gas and vapor cartridges need an **end-of-service-life indicator** or a written **change schedule** based on objective data. Odor is not an acceptable warning.

## Fit testing and seal checks

- Fit testing happens before first use, **at least every 12 months**, and whenever the worker's face changes in a way that could affect fit (for example, major weight change or dental work).
- **Qualitative fit tests** use a test agent the wearer can taste or smell (saccharin, Bitrex) or that causes irritation. **Quantitative fit tests** use an instrument to measure leakage numerically.
- Do a **user seal check** (positive and negative pressure) every time you put on a tight-fitting respirator.
- Facial hair that comes between the sealing surface and the face isn't allowed with tight-fitting respirators.

## Respirators and HAZWOPER PPE levels

Level A and B ensembles use SCBA or supplied air. Level C uses an APR, which is only allowed when contaminants and concentrations are known. Try the [respirator quiz](https://www.hazwoperosha.org/quizzes/respirator-quiz/).

## Frequently asked questions

### How often is respirator fit testing required?

Before first use, at least every 12 months, and whenever a change in the worker's face could affect the fit, under 29 CFR 1910.134(f).

### What is the assigned protection factor of a half-mask respirator?

Ten, for a filtering facepiece or elastomeric half-mask air-purifying respirator.

### How do you calculate maximum use concentration?

Multiply the respirator's assigned protection factor by the exposure limit of the contaminant. Never use an air-purifying respirator at or above IDLH.
