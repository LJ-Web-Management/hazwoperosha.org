Federal OSHA vs State-Plan States

HAZWOPER is a federal OSHA standard, but roughly half the states run their own OSHA-approved plan. Here's how that commonly affects your training requirements, with a closer look at Cal/OSHA.

See the State List OSHA Regulations by State →

Overview

Two Kinds of OSHA Jurisdiction

Federal OSHA States

Federal Enforcement

In states without their own approved plan, federal OSHA directly enforces 29 CFR 1910.120 (HAZWOPER) as written. Training requirements follow the federal standard without state-specific additions.

  • HAZWOPER training hours and tracks follow the federal standard
  • Enforcement and inspections are handled by federal OSHA
  • Most of the guidance in this library reflects the federal baseline
State-Plan States

State-Run Programs

State-plan states run their own OSHA-approved occupational safety program, which must be at least as protective as federal OSHA and can add requirements on top, including for HAZWOPER training.

  • Must meet or exceed federal HAZWOPER requirements
  • May add documentation, hours, or state-specific course content
  • California (Cal/OSHA) is the largest and most well-known example
ℹ️

State-plan status and requirements change over time. This page is a general educational overview, not a legal determination for any specific state — confirm current requirements with your state plan or a qualified safety professional before relying on it.

Spotlight

Cal/OSHA HAZWOPER Training

California runs one of the largest and most active state OSHA plans, and commonly gets its own dedicated HAZWOPER course track.

California

What's commonly different in California

Cal/OSHA (the Division of Occupational Safety and Health, part of California's Department of Industrial Relations) enforces its own HAZWOPER regulation, closely modeled on the federal standard but administered under state authority.

  • Cal/OSHA-specific 40-hour HAZWOPER course content, aligned to state regulatory language
  • Employers with California sites should confirm training is accepted under the Cal/OSHA plan specifically
  • Refresher and supervisor requirements commonly mirror the federal structure
